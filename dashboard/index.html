How the radar scores

The snapshot lists 758 protocols with a TVL (DefiLlama, 2026-11-14). 128 of them already have a token, and they teach the model. The other 342 have no token yet — no market cap, no CoinGecko id, no symbol — and those are the launches to watch.

The model is one comps curve, fitted live from the snapshot: mcap = a · TVL^b with a ≈ 1058, b ≈ 0.522, fitted by log-log OLS on the 128 priced protocols and refit every time the page builds. It is not a flat multiple: the mcap/TVL ratio collapses as protocols get bigger (0.65 below $10M TVL, 0.01 above $10B), so a flat multiple overvalues the giants and undervalues the small.

Bootstrap stability (200 seeded refits): the curve’s slope sits at 0.378–0.672 with 90% confidence, so the curve is not one lucky fit. The same 200 refits give every protocol a value band — low / central / high — and a rank range (P10–P90 of the rank across refits). The band is the fit’s own uncertainty: it says where the curve sits, not where any single launch will land. Individual launches scatter much wider — the leave-one-out median miss is 3.4× — and the dashboard does not hide that.

Launch odds come from a logistic model on log TVL, age, 7d growth and sector, fitted on all 758 protocols (pre-token = 0, launched = 1). Five-fold cross-validated accuracy is 64.2% against a 54.9% majority-class base rate (n=758) — modest, and stated as such. The odds are a resemblance score (“this protocol looks like the ones that already launched”), not a prediction of a future event.

Farm verdict — the farmer’s expected net, not the launch’s size. The expected airdrop slice is launch odds × ticket × (implied value / TVL) × a 7% airdrop share; the expected net is that slice minus gas. Capital is not a cost — the deposit comes back — so the cost is gas only, priced per transaction on the protocol’s own chain (estimates, Nov 2026: Ethereum $12.00, BSC $0.15, Arbitrum/Optimism $0.30, Base $0.10, Polygon $0.05, Solana $0.01, anything else $1.00 — 2 setup transactions + one weekly action + 2 claim transactions), over a 30–60 day hold (longer for younger protocols). The verdict is taken at the low end of the value band: FARM when the low-end expected net clears $10 at a $1,000 ticket, THIN when it is positive but below $10, SKIP when it is zero or negative. The $10 floor is the round number nearest the top decile of this board’s own expected-net distribution (p90 is $11.36) — a farm must clear $10 of expected profit, about $4 an hour of active work, not just break even. At the $1,000 ticket the split is 32 FARM / 74 THIN / 96 SKIP. Use the ticket toggle above to re-price every row at $10,000: the slice grows tenfold, the gas does not, and the verdicts move with it. Every plan also states its break-even — the share of the token supply your airdrop must be to cover the all-in cost.

Why the biggest launches are often SKIP. Rank is launch scale; the verdict is farm economics, and three forces pull them apart. First, gas: farming on Ethereum costs $108–$156 for the whole plan, which no $1,000-ticket slice can cover — all 54 Ethereum rows are SKIP at that ticket. Second, odds: the largest risk curators launch rarely, so their odds sit at 17–24%. Third, the slice multiple itself: the curve is sublinear, so a giant’s implied value is a thin sliver of its TVL (implied/TVL ≈ 0.005 at the top), and a $1,000 ticket buys a slice of a few cents. The board shows both numbers instead of hiding the tension — sort by “best farms” to see the rows where the economics actually work.

Coverage. The board ranks 202 protocols: the full 40-protocol house watchlist plus every other pre-token protocol that is a genuine launch candidate. Excluded, with reasons: centralised exchanges, liquid-staking / staking / bridge families (their token is a receipt or a wrapped asset), versioned deployments, wrapped assets. One watchlist member (Reti Pooling, a staking pool) enters through the watchlist, not the filter. The ledger above accounts for all 758.
